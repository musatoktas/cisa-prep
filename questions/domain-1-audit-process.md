# Domain 1: Information System Auditing Process

Original practice questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

---

**1.** An IS auditor is planning an audit of a payroll application. What should the auditor do FIRST?

- A. Select a sample of payroll transactions for testing
- B. Identify the key risks to the payroll process
- C. Review the access rights of payroll staff
- D. Interview the payroll manager about recent changes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Audit planning is risk-based. Understanding the key risks tells the auditor where to focus testing. Sampling, access reviews and interviews come after the risks and scope are understood.
</details>

---

**2.** Which of the following BEST supports the independence of the internal IS audit function?

- A. The audit team has strong technical skills in the systems being audited
- B. The chief audit executive reports functionally to the audit committee of the board
- C. Audit findings are agreed with IT management before the report is issued
- D. The audit plan is approved by the chief information officer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A functional reporting line to the audit committee protects audit from pressure by the management it audits. Approval of the audit plan by the CIO (D) would undermine independence. Agreeing findings with management (C) is good practice for accuracy but does not create independence.
</details>

---

**3.** During an audit, an IS auditor finds that a control was not working, and the IT manager offers to fix it before the report is issued. What should the auditor do?

- A. Remove the finding from the report once the fix is confirmed
- B. Report the finding and note that corrective action has been taken
- C. Delay the report until the fix has been in place for three months
- D. Help the IT manager design the fix to make sure it is effective

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The control failed during the audit period, so the finding stands. The report can note management's corrective action. Removing the finding (A) misrepresents the period audited. Designing the fix (D) impairs the auditor's independence.
</details>

---

**4.** Which type of evidence is generally the MOST reliable?

- A. A written statement from the system administrator that backups are tested monthly
- B. A backup test report produced by the IT operations team
- C. The auditor's own observation of a successful restore test
- D. Minutes of the IT steering committee confirming backup testing

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Evidence the auditor obtains directly is more reliable than evidence provided by the auditee. Written documents (B, D) are more reliable than oral or self-declared statements (A), but none beat direct observation.
</details>

---

**5.** An IS auditor wants to estimate the total value of errors in a large population of invoices. Which sampling method is MOST appropriate?

- A. Attribute sampling
- B. Variable sampling
- C. Discovery sampling
- D. Stop-or-go sampling

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Variable sampling estimates a monetary value or quantity. Attribute sampling (A) estimates the rate of a characteristic, for example how often an approval is missing. Discovery sampling (C) looks for at least one occurrence of a rare event. Stop-or-go (D) is a form of attribute sampling.
</details>

---

**6.** Increasing the required confidence level in a statistical sample will:

- A. Decrease the sample size
- B. Increase the sample size
- C. Have no effect on sample size
- D. Increase the tolerable error rate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Higher confidence requires more evidence, so the sample grows. A larger tolerable error would reduce the sample size, not the other way around.
</details>

---

**7.** The PRIMARY purpose of an audit charter is to:

- A. Document the audit procedures for each engagement
- B. Define the purpose, authority and responsibility of the audit function
- C. List the systems that will be audited this year
- D. Describe the qualifications of audit staff

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The charter establishes the audit function's mandate, authority, access rights and responsibilities, and is approved at board or audit committee level. Engagement procedures (A) and the annual plan (C) are separate documents.
</details>

---

**8.** While auditing user access, an IS auditor notices a possible fraud in an unrelated purchasing process that is outside the audit scope. What should the auditor do?

- A. Ignore it, because it is outside the agreed scope
- B. Expand the audit to investigate the fraud fully
- C. Report it promptly to an appropriate level of management or the audit committee
- D. Confront the employee involved to obtain an explanation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Significant issues found outside scope must not be ignored. They are communicated to the right level of management or the audit committee, who decide on any investigation. The auditor should not unilaterally expand scope (B) or alert a possible perpetrator (D).
</details>

---

**9.** Which audit technique is MOST suitable for continuously monitoring high-volume online transactions for unusual patterns?

- A. Test data
- B. Embedded audit modules
- C. Parallel simulation run once a year
- D. Walk-through of the transaction process

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Embedded audit modules (or similar continuous auditing tools) capture and flag transactions as they are processed. Test data (A) checks program logic with fictitious transactions. An annual parallel simulation (C) is not continuous. A walk-through (D) builds understanding but does not monitor.
</details>

---

**10.** An auditee disagrees with a finding in the draft report. What is the BEST course of action for the IS auditor?

- A. Remove the finding to maintain a good relationship
- B. Escalate immediately to the board
- C. Review the evidence again and, if the finding stands, include it with management's response
- D. Change the finding's risk rating to low

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The auditor checks the evidence and stays objective. If the evidence supports the finding, it stays in the report, and management's view is recorded alongside it. Removing or downgrading a finding to avoid conflict (A, D) breaks objectivity.
</details>
