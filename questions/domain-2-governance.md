# Domain 2: Governance and Management of IT

Original practice questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

---

**1.** Who is ultimately accountable for the governance of enterprise IT?

- A. The chief information officer
- B. The IT steering committee
- C. The board of directors
- D. The chief information security officer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The board is accountable for governance and sets direction. The CIO and steering committee manage and execute within that direction.
</details>

---

**2.** Which of the following BEST indicates that IT is aligned with the business?

- A. IT spending is below the industry average
- B. The IT strategic plan is derived from and traceable to the business strategy
- C. All IT projects are delivered on time
- D. IT has adopted a recognised framework such as COBIT

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Alignment means IT objectives support business objectives, which is shown by traceability from the business strategy to the IT plan. Low cost (A), on-time delivery (C) and framework adoption (D) do not prove alignment.
</details>

---

**3.** Who should be responsible for classifying a customer database?

- A. The database administrator
- B. The information security officer
- C. The data owner in the business
- D. The IT steering committee

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The data owner understands the business value and sensitivity of the data and is accountable for its classification and for approving access. The DBA (A) is a custodian who implements controls.
</details>

---

**4.** In a small IT team, the same person develops programs and moves them to production. Which control BEST compensates for this lack of segregation of duties?

- A. A formal job description for the developer
- B. Independent review of production change logs against approved change requests
- C. Requiring the developer to take mandatory annual leave
- D. Asking the developer to sign a code of conduct

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** An independent, detective review of what actually changed in production against what was approved compensates for the missing preventive control. Mandatory leave (C) can help detect fraud but is weaker and less direct.
</details>

---

**5.** An organisation outsources its data centre operations. What gives the IS auditor the MOST assurance about the provider's controls when a direct audit is not possible?

- A. The provider's marketing documents describing its security
- B. A recent independent service auditor's report covering operating effectiveness over a period (for example, SOC 2 Type 2)
- C. A signed confidentiality agreement
- D. The provider's ISO 9001 certificate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** An independent report that tests controls over a period gives evidence of operating effectiveness. A point-in-time design report would be weaker. A confidentiality agreement (C) and a quality certificate (D) do not cover the controls in question.
</details>

---

**6.** When reviewing an outsourcing contract, which clause is MOST important for the IS auditor?

- A. The provider's office locations
- B. The right to audit the provider or obtain independent assurance
- C. The provider's staff training plan
- D. The payment schedule

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Without a right to audit or to receive independent assurance, the organisation cannot verify the controls it remains accountable for.
</details>

---

**7.** Which of the following is a key RISK indicator rather than a key performance indicator?

- A. Percentage of projects delivered within budget
- B. Number of critical systems with unpatched high-severity vulnerabilities
- C. Average helpdesk response time
- D. Customer satisfaction score for IT services

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A KRI signals rising exposure to risk. The others measure performance against targets.
</details>

---

**8.** Management decides not to implement a recommended control because the cost exceeds the expected loss. This is an example of:

- A. Risk avoidance
- B. Risk transfer
- C. Risk acceptance
- D. Risk mitigation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Choosing to bear the risk after a cost-benefit decision is risk acceptance, which should be formally documented and approved by the right level of management within the risk appetite.
</details>

---

**9.** What should be in place FIRST when an organisation develops an information security programme?

- A. A firewall and intrusion detection system
- B. An information security policy approved by senior management
- C. A security awareness training course
- D. A security operations centre

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** An approved policy sets direction, scope and management commitment. Technical controls and training are implemented under the policy.
</details>

---

**10.** An IS auditor finds that IT policies are reviewed by IT but never approved by senior management. What is the GREATEST risk?

- A. Policies may contain spelling errors
- B. Policies may not reflect business objectives and may lack management support for enforcement
- C. Policies will be too long
- D. IT staff will not be able to find the policies

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Senior management approval shows that policies reflect business direction and gives them authority. Without it, enforcement and alignment are at risk.
</details>
