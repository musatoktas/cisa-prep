# CISA Prep: a 6-week plan at 4 hours a day

An open study plan for the ISACA **Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA)** exam. It is built for working professionals who can give the exam about **4 focused hours a day for 6 weeks**, roughly 160 hours in total.

I am using it for my own preparation. I am an ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor and a BSI Associate Tutor for ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001, so the plan leans on management-system thinking where it helps. It should work for anyone with some IT, security or audit background.

## The 4-hour day

Every study day uses the same shape, so you do not spend energy deciding what to do.

| Block | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 min | **Read**: the day's topic in the CISA Review Manual or the linked standard |
| 2 | 45 min | **Condense**: write a one-page summary in your own words and 10 flashcards |
| 3 | 90 min | **Practise**: 40 to 50 timed questions on the day's topic (about 1.5 minutes each) |
| 4 | 45 min | **Review**: read every explanation, including for the ones you got right, and log every miss in the tracker |

Day 7 of each week is lighter: 2 hours of mixed questions from all finished domains and a review of your wrong-answer log.

## How to think like a CISA

Most wrong answers come from answering as an engineer or a manager instead of as an auditor. A few rules that hold across the exam:

1. **You are the auditor, not the owner.** The auditor recommends and reports. Management decides and implements. An answer where the auditor fixes the problem is almost always wrong.
2. **Risk first.** When asked what to do *first*, look for the answer that understands or assesses risk before acting.
3. **Independence matters.** Any answer that compromises the auditor's independence or objectivity is wrong, even if it is efficient.
4. **Evidence must be sufficient, reliable and relevant.** Evidence from an independent third party or obtained directly by the auditor beats evidence provided by the auditee.
5. **Preventive beats detective, and detective beats corrective**, unless the question is about something that has already happened.
6. **Read the qualifier.** *Most*, *best*, *first*, *primary* and *greatest* change the right answer. Several options are often true. Pick the one that answers the exact question.

## Using this repository

1. Star or fork it, then copy [`tracker.md`](tracker.md) into your own notes.
2. Book the exam for the end of week 6 before you start. A fixed date keeps the plan honest.
3. Follow [`plan/week-1.md`](plan/week-1.md).
4. Use the questions here as a warm-up. The ISACA Questions, Answers and Explanations (QAE) database should be your main question source.

Corrections and new original questions are welcome as pull requests.
